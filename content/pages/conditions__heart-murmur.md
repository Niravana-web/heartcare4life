## What is a heart murmur?

A heart murmur is an extra swishing or whooshing sound a doctor hears through a stethoscope, made by turbulent blood flow in or near the heart. It is a sound, not a disease. Normal blood flow is smooth and quiet. When blood speeds up, squeezes through a narrowed valve or leaks backward through one that does not close, it becomes turbulent and audible. Murmurs are sorted into two groups. Innocent murmurs occur in structurally normal hearts and are common in children, in pregnancy, with fever, and with anemia or an overactive thyroid. Abnormal murmurs signal a real problem, most often a valve that has stiffened or started to leak. Doctors grade a murmur by loudness and describe when it occurs in the heartbeat, but the stethoscope alone cannot settle the question. As the [American Heart Association](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-valve-problems-and-disease) explains, an ultrasound of the heart is what distinguishes a harmless murmur from valve disease.

## Symptoms to watch for

Most murmurs cause no symptoms and are found during a routine exam. Symptoms usually mean the underlying valve problem has progressed.

Call the office if you have a known murmur along with:

- Shortness of breath with activity, or when lying flat
- Swelling in the ankles, feet or abdomen
- Fatigue or reduced stamina that is new for you
- [Palpitations](/conditions/heart-palpitations) or a persistently irregular pulse
- Lightheadedness with exertion
- Unexplained fevers, which can signal an infection of a valve

Call 911 if you have:

- Fainting, especially during exercise
- Chest pain or pressure lasting more than a few minutes
- Severe shortness of breath at rest, or waking gasping for air
- Coughing up pink, frothy sputum

Fainting during exertion with a known murmur can indicate severe narrowing of the aortic valve and requires emergency care. The [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/heartvalvediseases.html) overview of valve disease describes how these symptoms develop.

## How Dr. Nanavati diagnoses it

The exam is the starting point, and ultrasound gives the answer. Dr. Nanavati listens over each valve area, standing, sitting and with maneuvers that change how loud a murmur sounds.

- **[Transthoracic echocardiography](/services/transthoracic-echocardiography)** is the definitive test. It shows each valve moving in real time, measures how much blood leaks or how tight a narrowing is, and reports the pumping strength.
- **[Echocardiogram](/services/echocardiogram)** studies are performed in the office, take about 30 minutes and use no radiation.
- **[Transesophageal echocardiography](/services/transesophageal-echocardiography)** gives a much closer view when the standard study is inconclusive or when infection of a valve is suspected.
- **[Electrocardiogram (ECG or EKG)](/services/electrocardiogram-ecg-ekg)** checks for chamber enlargement and rhythm problems caused by a leaking or narrowed valve.
- **[Treadmill stress testing](/services/treadmill-stress-test)** measures your true exercise capacity when symptoms and valve measurements do not match.
- **[Holter monitoring](/services/holter-monitoring)** is added if you also have palpitations or suspected [atrial fibrillation](/conditions/atrial-fibrillation), which valve disease can cause.

## Treatment options

Many murmurs need nothing more than reassurance and a repeat listen. When a valve problem is confirmed, treatment follows its severity.

**Watchful waiting.** Mild to moderate valve disease is monitored with echocardiograms at set intervals, often yearly. The purpose is to catch the moment when the valve becomes severe but before the heart muscle is damaged. Keeping that schedule is the treatment.

**Risk factor management.** Blood pressure control reduces the load on a leaking valve. Cholesterol treatment guided by [advanced lipid testing](/services/advanced-lipid-testing) and the habits in [Life's Essential 8](https://www.heart.org/en/healthy-living/healthy-lifestyle/lifes-essential-8) protect the heart muscle overall. Good dental hygiene lowers the risk of a valve infection.

**Medication.** Diuretics relieve fluid buildup. Blood pressure medicines reduce strain. Rate control or anticoagulation is added if AFib develops. Medication does not fix a valve, but it manages symptoms and protects the heart.

**Repair or replacement.** Severe valve disease is treated with surgical repair, surgical replacement, or a catheter based valve procedure at an affiliated hospital. Dr. Nanavati handles the evaluation, timing and referral, then follows you afterward. [Cardiac rehabilitation](/services/cardiac-rehabilitation) supports recovery.

## What to expect at your visit

Bring the note or report that mentioned the murmur, your medication list and any previous echocardiogram results for comparison. Your appointment includes a full cardiac exam, blood pressure and an [electrocardiogram](/services/electrocardiogram-ecg-ekg). If an echocardiogram is ordered, you lie on your left side while a technologist moves a probe over your chest with gel. It is painless and involves no needles. Dr. Nanavati reviews the images with you and tells you plainly whether the murmur is innocent, and if not, which valve is involved and when the next study is due. The [FAQs page](/faqs) covers what to bring.

## When to see a cardiologist

Get evaluated if a clinician mentions a murmur you have never had checked, if a known murmur sounds louder, or if a murmur comes with breathlessness, swelling or fainting. Children with murmurs and adults with a bicuspid aortic valve or a family history of valve disease should also be assessed, as should anyone with an [aortic aneurysm](/conditions/aortic-aneurysm).
